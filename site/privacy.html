Privacy policy

Your vehicle records and stats stay on your device. AutoMate has no account or servers of its own. Maps, location lookups and purchases use Google services. Maps and document scanning may send technical data to Google. You can delete your local records anytime.

Last updated 22 November 2026

Who we are

This policy covers the AutoMate Android app (in.getautomate.app) and this website, getautomate.in. Questions about privacy: hello@getautomate.in.

What AutoMate stores, and where

Your vehicles, trips (routes, distance, times, stops), places you save, fill-ups, tolls, expenses, money received, garage records and reminders, photos and scans of bills and job cards, tags and your settings. All of it is saved in the app's private storage on your phone. Android's cloud backup is switched off for AutoMate, so none of it is copied to your Google account. AutoMate has no backend for these records.

What leaves your phone

Some features use Google services, which receive the data described below:

  • Maps. Trip maps are drawn with the Google Maps SDK, which downloads map tiles for the area on screen. The Maps SDK sends Google technical information such as device details, IP address, a Maps identifier and crash metrics. It may also send map interactions such as panning and zooming.
  • Addresses. To show “Home → Office” instead of coordinates, the start and end of a trip and where you parked are looked up with Android's geocoder, which asks Google.
  • Road routes. When you add or move a stop on a trip by hand, the points are sent to Google's Routes API to measure the drive between them.
  • Place search. When you search for a place while editing a trip, what you type and the trip's area are sent to Google Places to suggest matches.
  • What a payment stop was. When a payment arrives and the payee's name doesn't say what it was, AutoMate checks where the vehicle stood: first your saved places, then spots it has already learned, then the phone's geocoder. Only if none of those can tell does it send that stop's location to Google Places to ask whether it's a fuel pump, garage, car wash or parking. The answer is kept on the phone so the same spot isn't asked twice. The payment itself (amount, payee, message) is never sent.
  • Purchases. Buying AutoMate or subscribing goes through Google Play. Google handles the payment; AutoMate checks which products you own and acknowledges purchases through Google Play. AutoMate does not receive your card or bank details. As with any Play purchase, Google gives the developer an order record (order number, product, price, date and country).
  • Location and activity. Trip recording uses Google Play services' location and activity recognition on the phone.
  • Document scanner. Scanning a bill or job card uses Google's ML Kit document scanner, which works on the phone; ML Kit may send Google diagnostics about the scanner.

Google handles this data under the Google Privacy Policy. AutoMate has no developer-operated analytics or advertising. Google SDKs may collect technical and usage information as described above. All of these requests use encrypted connections (HTTPS).

Payment notifications

AutoMate doesn't ask for SMS permission. If you switch on Read payment notifications, Android's Notification access lets it read payment alerts as they arrive, so it can log FASTag tolls and keep other vehicle payments, such as fuel, for you to review. AutoMate shows what it reads and asks you to agree before it opens Android's settings.

  • What it reads. Only notifications from SMS apps (Google Messages, Samsung Messages), UPI apps (Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm, BHIM, CRED), bank and FASTag apps, and Gmail (the subject and preview only; AutoMate never gets access to your Gmail account). Android lets an app with notification access see every notification; AutoMate ignores every other app, and skips OTPs and offers.
  • What it keeps. FASTag tolls and parking are saved like a toll you add by hand: amount, balance, toll plaza, vehicle number, reference and time, without the message text. Other payments wait under To review and don't count as spend until you log them. A payment you don't log is deleted after the number of days you choose (1, 3 or 7; 7 by default), together with its message text; the text is also deleted as soon as you log or skip it.
  • New vehicle registrations. If a vehicle registration message arrives, AutoMate can offer to add that vehicle. Only the parsed details (registration number and dates) are kept, for up to 30 days, never the message text.
  • Where it goes. Nowhere. Payment notifications are read and kept on your phone only, and payments waiting for review aren't included in backups.
  • Turning it off. Switch off Read payment notifications in AutoMate's Settings, or remove AutoMate from Notification access in Android's settings.

Backups and sharing

An exported .automate backup holds everything AutoMate stores except payments waiting for review. You can protect it with a password. It goes wherever you save or send it; keep it somewhere private. Trip images and anything else you share go where you choose to share them.

How long data is kept

Your records stay on your phone until you delete them, clear the app's storage or uninstall the app. Unlogged payments are deleted after 1 to 7 days (your choice), and registration details after 30 days. Place answers from Google are refreshed after 30 days. See Delete your data.

This website

getautomate.in is a plain static site hosted on GitHub Pages. We do not set cookies or run analytics. The optional app-overview video loads YouTube’s privacy-enhanced player only after you choose to watch; until then, the preview is hosted on our site and no YouTube connection is made. When the player loads, YouTube receives connection information such as your IP address and may use storage or show ads under its own policies. See Google’s privacy policy. Closing the player unloads it. GitHub, as the host, may log visitors' IP addresses for security; see GitHub's privacy statement.

Children

AutoMate is meant for adults who own or drive a vehicle. It isn't aimed at children and doesn't knowingly collect anything from them.
